# Recording no-bat sounds

What to record when adding your own recordings to this set.

## Why own recordings help

- **Your own devices.** Detector, microphone and gain change what noise looks
  like. Sound effects come from studio microphones, so only recordings from
  real bat detectors (AudioMoth, Pipistrelle, Tascam with an ultrasonic mic,
  a USB mic on a phone) show what users will see.
- **Testing rules.** Most false bat calls from water and electrical noise in
  the sound effects are 1 to 3 ms long and 1 to 4 kHz wide. A rule based on
  that needs real recordings to check it against.
- **Training a classifier.** The insect false calls can't be told apart by
  simple rules; a learned classifier is the remaining option. It needs
  negatives from many places more than long recordings from a few.

## How long

1 to 5 minutes per place or source is enough. After that the false calls
per minute stop changing. Twenty 2-minute recordings in different places
are worth more than one 40-minute recording.

## When

In daylight, from about 2 hours after sunrise to 2 hours before sunset, when
bats are least likely to fly. Some bats fly in daylight (warm winter days,
the tropics, near roosts), so look at the spectrogram before adding a
recording.

## What to record

Most useful first, from what gives the most false bat calls so far:

1. **Water**: streams, weirs, waterfalls, fountains, dripping, waves on rocks
2. **Rain and wind**: rain on leaves, on the detector, on a tent; wind in
   grass, trees and reeds; wind on the microphone
3. **Insects**: bush-crickets and grasshoppers on summer afternoons, cicadas,
   bees at flowers, flies near the microphone
4. **Electrical**: LED and sodium streetlights, car park lights, power lines
   and substations, electric fences, ultrasonic pest repellers, parking
   sensors, motion sensors, alarms, a phone or laptop near the detector
5. **Vehicles**: traffic, tyres on a wet road, idling engines, trains, drones
6. **Handling**: footsteps on gravel and dry leaves, clothes rustling, keys,
   Velcro, strapping the detector to a tree, the detector in a pocket
7. **Birds and other animals**: high-pitched songbirds (wrens, goldcrests,
   finches), house sparrows, starlings; rodents and shrews if you find them
8. **People**: whispering, "s" sounds, breathing near the microphone

## What to note

For each recording: date, place, device, microphone, gain setting, sample
rate, and what you can hear or see. Name the file after the main sound
(`Stream over stones, AudioMoth medium gain.wav`).

Recordings for this public set need a licence that allows redistribution,
such as CC0 or CC BY 4.0.
